          to the CloudWatch native dashboard approach.
    \item \textbf{SQS three-tier fallback registry}: the SQS monitoring
          feature (added Jun~12) was accompanied by \textbf{960 automated
          tests} covering registry dispatch, fallback transitions, and queue
          metadata serialization.
\end{itemize}

The Discoverer Registry pattern --- in which a \texttt{(resource\_type, budget\_code)}
tuple maps to a concrete \texttt{BaseDiscoverer} subclass --- proved straightforward
to extend: adding a new platform or resource type required implementing one class
and registering one entry, with zero changes to routing, serialization, or test

via the Flask test client against all REST endpoints; and property-based tests using
the Hypothesis library, which generated random tag combinations to verify that the
two-stage filter never conflated \texttt{meridian} and \texttt{citcoworks}
resources regardless of input.

\section{Impact and Business Value}
\label{sec:impact_business_value}
% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The shift from a purely reactive to a proactively monitored infrastructure
is the central business value proposition of this internship's work. Two
measurable outcomes illustrate that shift most clearly.

\subsection{Incident Detection Improvement: MSK Consumer Lag}

The Apr~17, 2026 Meridian incident --- in which approximately 9 million
Kafka messages queued across the Action Processor consumer group, producing
a 7.5-hour processing delay affecting multiple prime-brokerage client funds ---
was the motivating event for the entire monitoring platform. The incident was
detected only because a fund administrator noticed that expected outputs had
not arrived; no automated alarm existed to catch the lag as it built.

The \texttt{EstimatedMaxTimeLag} alarm deployed as part of IISS-487 directly
addresses this gap. Before deployment, the detection pathway required a human
to notice missing downstream outputs and escalate through Tier~1 business
support to the IA-SRE team --- a latency of \textbf{2--3 minutes at best,
and hours at worst} when the delay began outside working hours. After
deployment, the CloudWatch alarm fires within \textbf{30 seconds} of
\texttt{EstimatedMaxTimeLag} crossing its configured threshold, routing a
notification directly to the IA-SRE team's SNS topic. This improvement from a
multi-minute manual detection process to a sub-30-second automated alert
represents a qualitative step change in the team's ability to contain
fund-impacting incidents before they reach business-level severity.

\subsection{Operational Efficiency: Fleet Visibility}

Prior to my internship, the IA-SRE team had no single view of all Lambda
functions, ECS clusters, and EC2 instances under their operational mandate.
Resource discovery was ad hoc --- when a new function was deployed, a
Confluence page might be updated, but the update was manual and frequently
lagged the deployment by days or weeks.

The combination of the Grafana dashboard and the inventory API delivers two
distinct efficiency improvements. First, the Grafana workspace provides a
continuously refreshed view of resource health across all six platforms, eliminating the need to navigate six separate AWS console tabs
to assess the current state of the IA platform. Second, the inventory API
provides a machine-readable endpoint that downstream tooling --- including the
Grafana resource inventory panel on Row~13 of the dashboard --- can query
programmatically, enabling future automation such as automated tag compliance
checks and dynamic alarm threshold recalibration.

The Container Insights enablement on May~11 delivered an additional operational
improvement: ECS task-level CPU and memory telemetry that had been entirely
absent became available within hours of the \texttt{UpdateClusterSettings}
call, immediately surfacing a previously invisible CPU saturation pattern on
one of the Meridian Action Processor services.

\subsection{RPA Support Value}

The four support rotation weeks contributed operational value beyond their
primary purpose of keeping production bots running. Each rotation produced
a written incident summary (filed in the team's Confluence knowledge base)
and, in the case of IISS-706, a post-incident design change: my CAIS
hotfix analysis revealed that the module-import credential caching pattern
used by the affected bot was fragile under Secrets Manager rotation cycles
that returned empty strings during the rotation window. I elevated this finding
to the team as a platform-wide architectural concern, informing
future RPA credential-handling guidelines.
