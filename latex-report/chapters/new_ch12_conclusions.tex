\subsection{Summary of Outcomes}

This internship delivered a reactive-to-proactive transformation of the IA
platform's observability posture across three distinct work streams. The
Grafana AMG centralized dashboard (IISS-487) replaced an entirely manual
monitoring process with a continuously refreshed, alarm-backed, 110-panel
workspace covering every service category in the IA infrastructure. The
CloudWatch monitoring platform (IISS-455/469/482--486) catalogued the full
AWS service fleet, produced a reusable IaC alarm provisioner, and resolved a
fundamental tag ambiguity that had been silently skewing resource inventory
counts. The inventory REST API (IISS-507) wrapped that fleet discovery logic
into a tested, extensible service layer that downstream tooling can consume
programmatically. These three streams were complementary rather than
sequential: each fed information into the others, with the inventory API's
discovery logic underpinning the Grafana dashboard's resource enumeration,
and the Grafana alarms surfacing gaps in the inventory's coverage.

Against the learning goals set at the outset, all objectives were met. AWS
service proficiency was achieved at production depth across CloudWatch,
Lambda, ECS, MSK, SQS, S3, Secrets Manager, and CloudFormation. SRE
principles were applied concretely: the four golden signals were mapped to
real metric namespaces, SLO targets were articulated for each monitored
service, and the MSK consumer lag alarm reduced the detection window for a
category of incidents that had previously cost hours of undetected fund
impact. Python development progressed from scripted analysis to a
production-quality API with 99\% automated test coverage. The Kiro IDE's
spec-driven workflow accelerated my design-to-code velocity across the inventory
API and this report itself --- providing practical experience with AI-native
engineering in a regulated financial services environment.

The following chapters (\ref{chap:achievements}--\ref{chap:future_work}) present the detailed evidence for each of these outcomes: Chapter~\ref{chap:achievements} covers deliverables and impact, Chapter~\ref{chap:challenges} documents the engineering obstacles, and Chapter~\ref{chap:future_work} outlines the planned next steps.

\subsection{Personal Reflection}

Looking back across five months of full-time engineering in a financial services
SRE context, what stands out most is how profoundly the work changed the mental
model I brought to reliability problems. The part-time onboarding phase in March
and Apr introduced an environment where production failures were detected by
business users noticing missing outputs --- a model that, from the outside, seemed
merely inconvenient. The Meridian Incident reframed that impression sharply:
nine million queued Kafka messages and a 7.5-hour processing delay affecting
client funds is not an inconvenience; it is a service reliability failure of
exactly the kind that SRE practice exists to prevent. Building the instrumentation
that would have caught that incident in under thirty seconds, and then watching
the MSK consumer lag alarm arm itself and begin monitoring live production
traffic, gave a concreteness to ``observability'' that no course module had
provided.

The broader lesson from my internship is that SRE is not a set of tools --- it is
a discipline of making implicit system behaviors explicit and measurable. The
Grafana dashboard, the alarm provisioner, and the inventory API are artifacts of
that discipline applied to one team's infrastructure. Each one makes something
previously invisible visible: which Lambda functions are erroring, which ECS
services are approaching CPU saturation, which Kafka consumer groups are falling
behind. That act of making things visible is the foundation on which every
subsequent improvement --- auto-healing, anomaly detection, capacity forecasting ---
must be built. Arriving at that understanding through the work, rather than reading
about it, is the internship's most durable outcome.

The technical skills accumulated across these five months --- production-level
fluency with AWS observability services, IaC authoring under real deadline
pressure, Python API development with a property-based test suite, and
AI-augmented engineering with the Kiro IDE --- are immediately applicable to
the renewal period and to any future SRE or platform engineering role. The
non-technical skills --- production incident response under time pressure,
cross-timezone stakeholder communication, and the discipline of writing things
down in the form others can act on --- turned out to matter just as much.
